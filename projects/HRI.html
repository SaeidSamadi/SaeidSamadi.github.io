Human-Robot Interaction

Nov. 2018 - Feb. 2022

Touch Semantics for Intuitive Physical Manipulation of Humanoids

This project focuses on creating a novel touch-based interaction protocol for humanoid robots to allow intuitive physical manipulation by humans. The research introduces the "Two-Touch Kinematic Chain Paradigm" (2TKCP), a set of rules for interpreting touch-based intentions. These rules enable users to manipulate the robot's joints or perform high-level whole-body tasks, such as repositioning, stepping, and walking, by exerting force through two touchpoints.

The method integrates capacitive touch sensors on the robot's shell and force-torque sensors on its limbs to detect human interaction. Using these inputs, the robot interprets touch intentions, allowing intuitive repositioning and task control. This approach eliminates the need for explicit programming, making it accessible for non-experts. The project further validates its methodology through a user study, showing that even novices can manipulate robots intuitively.

  • Developed the "Two-Touch Kinematic Chain Paradigm" (2TKCP).
  • Enabled intuitive manipulation using capacitive and force-torque sensors.
  • Validated methodology through user studies with non-experts.

For more information, refer to the publication in IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems. [View Paper]
