Case study · 2021

Touch-Based Human–Robot Interaction

A physical interaction framework that translates two touch points into intuitive commands for joint-level and whole-body humanoid motion.

  • HRP-4
  • Capacitive sensing
  • Force/torque sensing
  • User study

Touch semantics for intuitive manipulation

Challenge

Programming or teleoperating a high-degree-of-freedom humanoid is difficult for non-specialists. The project asked whether a person could instead communicate motion intent by touching and physically guiding the robot in a natural way.

Approach

The Two-Touch Kinematic Chain Paradigm (2TKCP) defines rules for interpreting a pair of physical contacts. Capacitive sensors identify where the robot is touched, while force/torque measurements capture the direction and intensity of guidance.

  • Mapped two touch points to a relevant kinematic chain and motion intent.
  • Supported direct joint repositioning and higher-level whole-body behaviours.
  • Combined shell touch sensing with limb force/torque feedback.
  • Evaluated the interaction with non-expert users.

Outcome

Participants could guide repositioning, stepping, and walking behaviours without explicit robot programming, demonstrating a practical interface for physical collaboration.
