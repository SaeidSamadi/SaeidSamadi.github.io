Real-Time Whole-Body Balance Control

Nov. 2019 - Oct. 2021

Humanoid Control Under Interchangeable Fixed and Sliding Unilateral Contacts

This project focuses on developing a robust control strategy for humanoid robots operating in challenging multi-contact scenarios. The approach enables the robot to switch seamlessly between fixed and sliding contact modes, maintaining active balance and dynamic stability. By using a novel optimization technique based on the Chebyshev center, this method calculates the safe center-of-mass (CoM) position and wrench distribution in real-time, eliminating the need for precomputed balance regions. The framework integrates sliding and fixed contacts into a unified control approach, which can adapt to non-coplanar and complex terrains.

Goals

  • Enable humanoid robots to maintain balance while transitioning between fixed and sliding contacts in real-time.
  • Provide a computationally efficient control framework for tasks involving multi-contact motion.
  • Demonstrate advanced capabilities like shuffling, co-wiping, and transitions in non-coplanar settings.

Experiments

The approach was validated on the HRP-4 humanoid robot, which successfully performed complex tasks, such as wiping surfaces, shuffling on inclined planes, and maintaining stability during transitions.

Key Outcomes

  • Reduction in computational cost for balance control.
  • Seamless integration of contact modes into a single control framework.
  • Improved stability and adaptability in confined spaces.

For more information, refer to the publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters.

Jan. 2019 - Oct. 2019

Balance of Humanoid Robots in a Mix of Fixed and Sliding Multi-Contact Scenarios

This project addresses the challenge of maintaining the balance of humanoid robots in multi-contact settings with both fixed and intentionally sliding contacts. The proposed methodology introduces a Center-of-Mass (CoM) Support Area (CSA) that ensures stability during such interactions. The CSA is computed analytically and used as a constraint in a Centroidal Quadratic Program (QP) to optimize the CoM position and wrench distribution under multi-contact scenarios. This approach is demonstrated in tasks such as pushing against a wall and wiping with the HRP-4 humanoid robot. The methodology enables robust balance control for tasks requiring sliding contacts, expanding the capabilities of humanoid robots in dynamic and interactive environments.

Key highlights of the project include:

  • Introduction of a CSA for multi-contact scenarios with sliding contacts.
  • Use of Centroidal QP for balancing and wrench distribution.
  • Experimental validation on the HRP-4 robot in pushing and wiping tasks.

Oct. 2020 - Jan. 2021

Control of Humanoid in Multiple Fixed and Moving Unilateral Contacts

This project addresses the challenge of maintaining balance in multi-limbed humanoid robots, such as the HRP-4, during complex tasks involving multiple fixed and moving unilateral contacts. The key innovation lies in enhancing the computational efficiency of geometric balance region algorithms, making them feasible for real-time integration into whole-body control frameworks. By computing balance regions dynamically and incorporating them directly into task-level control, this approach avoids the traditional two-level computation pitfalls, ensuring smoother and more adaptive movements in humanoids.

Experimental validation involved HRP-4 performing tasks such as pushing a box while maintaining balance, demonstrating the feasibility of real-time control under challenging contact dynamics. The results highlight significant improvements in the robot's ability to adapt its center of mass (CoM) and contact forces dynamically.
