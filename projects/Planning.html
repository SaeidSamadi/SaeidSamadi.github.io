Foot Step Planning

Nov. 2018 - Feb. 2022

NAS: N-step Computation of All Solutions to the Footstep Planning Problem

This project presents NAS, an innovative algorithm designed to compute the entire set of feasible solutions for humanoid robot footstep planning problems. NAS introduces a globally optimal policy capable of efficiently querying real-time plans, enabling robots to plan and adapt footsteps in dynamic environments. Despite the theoretical exponential complexity, the framework optimizes computations to achieve a manageable bilinear complexity, supporting GPU parallelization.

The method is validated through extensive simulations and hardware experiments on the Talos humanoid robot, showcasing robust performance in various scenarios, including minimum-step locomotion planning and dynamic re-planning.

Future advancements aim to reduce computation times further and expand the algorithm's applicability to more complex motion planning scenarios.

For more information, refer to the publication presented at the IEEE-RAS 23rd International Conference on Humanoid Robots, Nancy, France, 2024. [Paper Link]

Nov. 2018 - Feb. 2022

Online Multicontact Receding Horizon Planning via Value Function Approximation

This project focuses on enabling legged robots, such as humanoid robots, to plan multicontact motions in a receding horizon manner. By introducing novel approaches to approximate value functions, this work significantly reduces computation complexity, allowing real-time replanning of motions even in dynamic and uneven terrains. Two methods were proposed:

  • Multi-Fidelity RHP (MF-RHP): Combines accurate dynamics modeling in the execution horizon with relaxed dynamics in the prediction horizon, ensuring efficient and feasible planning.
  • Locally Guided RHP (LG-RHP): Uses a learned oracle to predict intermediate goals, constructing local value functions that guide efficient short-horizon planning.

Key contributions include extensive simulations validating the feasibility of planned trajectories, as well as real-world experiments on the Talos humanoid robot, demonstrating the effectiveness of the LG-RHP approach.

Goals: To enable real-time multicontact motion planning for humanoid robots traversing challenging terrains while ensuring computational efficiency and adaptability to dynamic environments.
